# Submission disclosures and guide review

ASTRA-SHIELD is a **team-designed and team-developed prototype**, supported by SI tools to accelerate development and testing. The team supplied the project brief, product and measurement requirements, frontend design and implementation, backend work, and clinical-alert proposals. The disclosures here identify assistance within that process; they do not replace the team's authorship, decisions or responsibility.

This review uses the uploaded **“NASA Space Apps Challenge.pdf”**, titled **Project Submission Guide**, dated **November 13, 2026**, with 16 PDF pages. Page numbers below refer to the PDF pages. Its SHA-256 is recorded in [PROVENANCE.json](../PROVENANCE.json). This is a review of the supplied guide and repository, not confirmation that the team's submission page has been completed or accepted.

## Suggested NASA-resource description

> We used NASA Human Research Program's 5 Hazards of Human Spaceflight and Risks of Human Spaceflight to frame the sleep, behavioral-health and cardiovascular context explained in our dashboard. NASA's Reaction Self Test background motivated the reaction-time demonstration. Our simulator's source manifest also cites NASA Standard Measures and ARCHER for consistent personal observations and activity/sleep/behavioral monitoring concepts. Ongoing HRP Research provided general challenge background. These resources informed the problem and measurement context; all numerical records, labels and journal examples in the training cohort were generated by our own uncalibrated simulator. NASA did not supply those records or validate our coefficients, alert thresholds or models. Our task is a custom 60-response demonstration, not a validated NASA/ISS PVT protocol.

Use the [main README's NASA resource links](../README.md#nasa-resources-used) with that description. The challenge also supplied CSA resources about [radiation monitoring](https://www.asc-csa.gc.ca/eng/news/articles/2026/2026-02-26-monitoring-radiation-astronaut-health.asp) and [science in space](https://www.asc-csa.gc.ca/eng/iss/science/why-do-we-conduct-science-experiments-in-space.asp). They are available background references; no CSA measurement dataset or radiation model is used in the shipped app. Do not imply otherwise in the submission.

## Testing and current scope

The integrated project is tested, and testing continues as the prototype develops. Recorded verification includes **80 UI checks, 48 end-to-end integration checks, 25 frontend regression tests, 16 backend tests, and 19 native Windows checks for each of the portable and installed executables**. The inference-only Windows export was compared with the original selected models on 130 cases; six reference cases were also checked on Windows. See the [verification reports](../integration-reports/README.md).

These tests establish the recorded software behavior and numerical portability. They do not establish medical performance. The model uses synthetic data; **Macro-F1 0.5273 is not accuracy**. The original model and CSV remain intact, with the same five selected models in the Windows deployment export. The five Alert demo presets are presentation examples; the full Clinical Alert V2 engine and its 14 medical scenarios remain deferred. Attention accuracy is manual input. Interactive testing on the team's actual Windows laptop and any future clinical validation are separate from the checks already completed.

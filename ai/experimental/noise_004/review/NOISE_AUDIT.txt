ASTRA-SHIELD — INDEPENDENT TRAINING-DATA NOISE AUDIT
Date: 2026-11-14

Finding
There is deliberate stochastic variation in the synthetic generator, but this
audit found no demonstrated corrupt measurements or incorrectly assigned fatigue
labels that justify deleting observations. Extreme values are not synonymous
with erroneous values. The proposed robust-tail rule primarily identifies the
intentional physiological anomaly examples.

Scope and provenance
Only data/train.csv was opened: 5,040 observations, 84 virtual participants, 60
daily rows each, 46 columns. Source code and task definitions were also inspected.
No validation, final-test, full prepared dataset, or raw calibration/outcome file
was opened. No model was fitted, no row was changed, and the training-file hash
remains a9b7aba9497fbcf68b6f5bb771b01dbf82c3b00c93a2a0c6873f705712a235e5.
All measurements are from an uncalibrated simulator, not real astronaut records.

What was checked again
- Zero missing or non-finite values, exact duplicate rows, duplicate person/day
  keys, duplicate full predictor vectors, or violations of documented bounds.
- Zero mismatches in the four baseline-relative feature formulas beyond their
  declared rounding precision. The four baselines stay fixed within each person.
- The prior-three-day features can be reconstructed from the training file for
  4,788 rows per feature. All agree within rounding tolerance. The first three
  days per participant require earlier calibration records, which were not read.
- Tomorrow's fatigue score agrees with the next recorded day's current score in
  all 4,956 checkable adjacent-day pairs. The last day needs an outcome beyond this
  training table and was not independently reconstructed here.
- Every fatigue class agrees with its stated future-score cutoffs of 4 and 7.
- Integer counts, attention correct/trial ratios, sleep opportunity, and the
  non-overlapping 24-hour activity budget are internally consistent.
- Attention accuracy is correctly quantized in 2.5-point steps for 40 trials.
  Such steps, repeated baselines, and bounded counts are not noise to smooth away.

Feature redundancy is a representation choice, not corrupt observations. Current
sleep, baseline sleep, and sleep delta contain an exact arithmetic relationship;
HR and HRV have analogous identities, and reaction-time percent change is derived
from current and baseline reaction time. Dropping a redundant predictor can be
tested as feature selection without deleting rows. It does not prove that the
discarded measurements are scientifically irrelevant. Independent clipping or
random perturbation of such linked columns would instead break their consistency.

Noise that is deliberately built into the simulator
data/source_generator.py adds a latent fatigue innovation with SD 0.62 and a
fatigue self-report term with SD 0.65, followed by clipping and rounding. It also
adds stress, mood, resting-HR, HRV, trial-level reaction-time, and future schedule
variation. These distributions were not calibrated to measured human biology.

There is no saved per-row latent truth or measurement-error realization in the
training CSV that would identify which observed self-report is an error. The
target is tomorrow's observed self-reported fatigue, not latent noiseless fatigue.
Smoothing or replacing that target would change the prediction task. Tomorrow's
fresh random perturbations also cannot be recovered from today's inputs.

The future score lies within 0.5 points of a class cutoff in 1,615 rows (32.04%),
and within 1 point in 3,250 rows (64.48%). Today's and tomorrow's classes differ
in 1,871 rows (37.12%); continuous-score correlation is 0.5096. These describe a
noisy and only partly predictable task, not 1,871 incorrect labels. Deleting hard
or boundary-near outcomes would preferentially make the problem easier and alter
its evaluation distribution.

What the proposed outlier rule actually selects
For descriptive context, medians and MADs were computed on the whole TRAIN table
for sleep delta, HR delta, HRV delta, and reaction-time percent change. Flagging
any absolute robust z-score > 4.5 gives:
- 254 of 5,040 rows flagged (5.04%).
- HR delta: 249 flags; HRV delta: 39; sleep delta and reaction-time change: zero.
- Every flagged row is one of the deliberately injected physiological events.
- There are 324 such event rows overall (6.43% of the training table).
- Thus the descriptive rule would select 78.40% of those intended event examples.
- Among the 254 flags, next-day classes are 44 Low, 187 Moderate, and 23 High.

The event annotation was examined only after computing the input-only flags; it
did not decide which rows were flagged. The experiment must fit median/MAD anew
inside each fitting fold. These whole-TRAIN numbers are not CV preprocessing.

The generator deliberately adds 13..24 bpm to HR and scales HRV by 0.45..0.72 on
event days. Those examples are important for the separate anomaly-detection use
case. Removing them from that task would remove the very examples the project
wants to detect. A fatigue-classifier sensitivity experiment can still determine
whether suppressing these events during fitting helps or harms fatigue forecasts.

Other descriptive outlier definitions give different answers
Across 15 current measurements, a conventional 1.5-IQR rule flags 727 rows
(14.42%), including 140 of the 550 High next-day cases (25.45%). A 3-IQR rule flags
45 rows. Neither rule proves that any flagged measurement is wrong. This also
shows why a fixed cleaning deletion percentage is not scientifically justified.
The complete per-feature tail table is descriptive_feature_tails.csv.

Generator artifacts and repetition
Some observations equal generator bounds: for example, exercise is zero on 80
days, sleep midpoint reaches the +180-minute cap on 8 days, and fatigue report
reaches 10 on 3 days. These follow explicit clipping rules; they are simulator
artifacts to disclose, not evidence of malformed data that should be deleted.
All 5,040 full journal strings are unique, but there are only 12 opening-sentence
templates. This is limited language diversity and synthetic construction, not a
reason to delete otherwise valid physiological observation rows. Improving the
journal component requires representative independently annotated language.

Assessment of the parent's bounded experiment
The planned fixed-RF comparison is methodologically appropriate as a sensitivity
analysis: control versus FIT-only exclusion or 0.25 weight for robust-tail rows;
fit-only median/MAD; identical original participant folds and rows for evaluation;
all held-out tails retained; additional forward-time evaluations; no final-test
access and no automatic model or dataset replacement.

It preserves algebraic feature relationships because it does not independently
clip current, baseline, delta, and history columns. Report how many rows each
fitting fold excludes/downweights, class composition, and the held-out event
subgroup results. The main foreseeable harm is poorer predictions on genuine
physiological deviations. Even a small aggregate F1 gain would not establish that
the flagged rows were invalid or that the source dataset should be rewritten.

No recommendation to delete source rows follows from this descriptive audit.
Wait for the fixed comparison and its uncertainty/temporal checks before deciding
whether any alternate training recipe is worth further development.

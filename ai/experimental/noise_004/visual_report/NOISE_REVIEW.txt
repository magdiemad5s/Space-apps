ASTRA-SHIELD — NOISE CHECK AND CLEANING EXPERIMENT
Reviewed 2026-11-14. Original longitudinal synthetic dataset only.

DECISION
Keep the original dataset and official model. The data includes deliberately
simulated random variation, but this audit did not identify corrupted records.
Experimental exclusion produced a small positive development result, not enough
evidence to adopt it or to label excluded records as errors.

WHAT WAS CHECKED
Only the 5,040-row training partition (84 virtual participants) was opened for
observation-level analysis. No validation or final-test partition was opened.
Missing/non-finite values, duplicates, documented bounds, measurement arithmetic,
fixed baselines, reconstructable prior means and next-day target linkage passed
the specified checks. This does not establish scientific validity or absence of
every possible type of noise. The underlying simulator is not calibrated to real
astronaut measurements.

WHAT LOOKS LIKE NOISE
1. The generator deliberately adds fatigue variation and self-report error-like
   random terms. Their respective standard deviations are 0.62 and 0.65 in the
   custom score units. These are simulator parameters, not estimates from humans.
   The target is tomorrow's observed simulated self-report, not hidden fatigue.
   The stochastic terms do not identify particular records that should be deleted.
2. There are 324 intentionally injected current HR/HRV events. A descriptive
   robust rule flags 254 of 5,040 rows (5.04%); every flagged row is one of those
   events. These examples represent unusual physiology by design. An outlier
   flag is not proof that a measurement is wrong.
3. 1,615 future fatigue scores are within 0.5 of a class cutoff. Their labels
   match the declared score rules. Deleting or relabeling these difficult cases
   would change the forecasting task rather than repair an established error.
4. Attention scores in 2.5-point steps follow a 40-trial protocol. Repeated
   personal baselines and exact current/baseline/delta identities are expected.

CONTROLLED EXPERIMENT
The fixed 26-feature Random Forest was compared with two fitting treatments:
exclude extreme fitting rows, or retain them with sample weight 0.25. A row was
flagged if any of four baseline-relative features had absolute robust z > 4.5.
Medians and median absolute deviations were fitted separately within every
fitting subset. No future target or event annotation was used to make a flag.
No feature values or target labels were changed. Every original held-out row was
scored by every candidate. The anomaly detector was not retrained.

All candidates used the same three participant folds and all 5,040 observations:
  Treatment                     Mean grouped-CV Macro-F1
  Unchanged Random Forest       0.51630
  Exclude flagged fitting rows  0.51986
  Weight flagged rows 0.25      0.51645
  Current-fatigue persistence   0.51400

These are development cross-validation scores. They are not the earlier 0.5273
validation score and are not directly comparable with the 4,452-row history study.

Exclusion improved mean Macro-F1 by 0.00356. Its two forward-time differences
were +0.00411 and +0.01046, with all evaluation rows retained. This is a small
positive signal. However, the paired participant-bootstrap 95% interval for
the pooled out-of-fold difference was [-0.00402, +0.01144], including zero.
Weighting improved mean Macro-F1 by only 0.00015 and worsened both time checks;
its pooled-difference interval was [-0.00704, +0.00720]. Neither candidate met
the prespecified +0.01 mean improvement requirement.

The intervals describe reused synthetic development predictions, not a new
independent test. The small exclusion result does not prove no benefit exists,
and does not establish the flagged data as corrupt. It could reflect reduced
influence of current physiological events on the separate next-day fatigue task.

ACTUAL FITTING COUNTS
Group fold 0: 174 of 3,360 fitting rows excluded; 3,186 retained.
Group fold 1: 162 of 3,360 fitting rows excluded; 3,198 retained.
Group fold 2: 169 of 3,360 fitting rows excluded; 3,191 retained.
Each candidate still scored all 1,680 held-out rows in each fold.
All flagged fitting rows were injected events in every group/time stage.
These are temporary experimental fitting subsets, not a cleaned replacement CSV.

GRAPH INTERPRETATION
PDF page 1: the separate HR/HRV cluster consists of deliberate injected events.
The fatigue plot shows real variation in the simulated forecasting target: similar
current scores can be followed by different next-day scores. Neither plot supplies
a reliable rule for deciding that an individual record is incorrect.
PDF page 2: the small gains are visible beside fold variation. Exclusion improves
both time checks, while weighting worsens them. The source dataset is retained.

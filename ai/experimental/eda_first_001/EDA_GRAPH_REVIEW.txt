ASTRA-SHIELD — EDA-FIRST GRAPH REVIEW AND FEATURE DECISIONS

Review scope
The three existing training-only PNG charts were opened and visually reviewed,
along with numeric_summary.csv, high_correlations.csv, target_class_counts.csv,
EDA_REPORT.txt, the data dictionary, and the feature allowlist. No test.csv or
validation metric report was opened to make these recommendations. This review
precedes the revised development experiment; it does not retroactively change
the chronology of the initial baseline run.

1. Training feature distributions
Chart: reports/eda/training_feature_distributions.png.
The axes and units are readable and consistent with the dictionary. Sleep is
roughly unimodal (mean 6.905 h, observed 3.433–9.8 h). Resting HR has a main mass
around 60–85 bpm and a smaller right tail, reaching 106.717 bpm. RMSSD spans
8.952–74.934 ms with no zero values in the numeric summary. Median reaction time
is about 330.6 ms (range 238.732–428.2 ms). Custom stress has a right tail; custom
fatigue is centered near 5.1 and bounded by the custom 0–10 scale.
These are plausible simulator shapes under its definitions, not evidence that
the simulator matches astronaut physiology. A rare value is not automatically
invalid. Do not trim the HR tail or winsorize valid sleep/cognitive values merely
to make histograms more Gaussian. Random Forest and XGBoost do not require normal
input distributions, so no normality-driven scaling/transform is prescribed.

2. Category distribution
Chart: reports/eda/training_class_distribution.png.
Moderate: 3,411 (67.68%); Low: 1,079 (21.41%); High: 550 (10.91%).
The majority category dominates. Plain accuracy would be a poor primary selection
criterion. Use validation Macro-F1, balanced training weights, and explicit
per-class precision/recall. Compare with both majority and current-fatigue
persistence baselines. Do not rebalance the combined train/validation/test table.
The graph shows 5,040 records but only 84 independent virtual people. Keep every
person's days together and do not remove their repeated ID as a duplicate.

3. Correlation heatmap
Chart: reports/eda/numeric_correlation_heatmap.png.
The heatmap shows intentional baseline/current/delta/history blocks. Two absolute
Pearson correlations exceed 0.85: sleep duration versus sleep duration delta
(r=0.919637), and fixed HR baseline versus prior-three-day absolute HR mean
(r=0.892748). Other visible blocks link raw HRV or reaction time to their fixed
personal levels. Several current behavioral/sleep indicators covary, as the
simulator was designed to do. No correlation here proves a medical mechanism,
causal direction, or future target relevance. Tree models can retain correlated
features, although SHAP/permutation attribution may be spread across them.

4. Prespecified feature comparison
Keep the existing full26 candidate. Compare it against a reduced17 personalized
candidate BEFORE running any new fits. The reduced candidate keeps absolute
sleep duration and a fixed sleep reference, sleep continuity/timing, past-only
sleep mean, current self-reports/work context, HR/RMSSD baseline deltas, relative
reaction-time change, lapse count and attention accuracy. It omits the raw and
fixed-level cardiovascular/cognitive block, absolute HR/reaction-time histories,
and the sleep delta that is highly correlated with retained absolute sleep.
This tests a simpler personalized representation. It does not assert that omitted
information has zero predictive value. All 26 individual keep/drop rationales and
exact feature lists are in FEATURE_DECISIONS.json.

The 17 predictors still require the historical calibration measurements used to
compute their relative changes. Fewer predictors do not imply fewer sensors or
that the backend can fabricate a missing baseline. Current fatigue self-report is
kept for both feature sets: it is available today and the target concerns tomorrow.
Its value should be assessed against a persistence baseline, not called leakage
solely because it shares a name with the future outcome. Future labels remain
excluded from both sets.

5. Cleaning and causal engineering decisions
The prepared training source already passed its defined missingness, nonfinite,
range, duplicate, and sleep/time consistency checks. Retain all rows; no removal
or replacement is justified by these graphs. Keep constant metadata and redundant
protocol counts excluded through explicit allowlists. The defensive imputer must
fit only on training data; it does not imply missing cells were actually present.
Fixed seven-day calibration means, current-versus-baseline changes and t-3..t-1
means are already engineered and source-tested. Do not recalculate baselines from
all 60 mission days or include current/future rows in historical means.

6. Next-stage protocol
Freeze this review and FEATURE_DECISIONS.json before new training. Fit the two
fixed algorithm recipes on each of the two feature sets using training only.
Compare their validation Macro-F1 and per-class precision/recall. Report the four
candidates and baselines, not only the winner. The existing test cohort has already
been evaluated in the initial experiment. Exclude it from this development revision
and make no claim of a new independent test result. Preserve the delivered initial
artifact and historical report; any revised candidate is development-only until
there is genuinely independent evaluation data.
